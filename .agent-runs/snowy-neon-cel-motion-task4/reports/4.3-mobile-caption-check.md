### One-line fit

All three cues render on a **single line**. The longest cue (12) occupies 190.7 CSS px of the 390 px viewport width — well within the ~335 px usable width at 7 % side margins. Visual confirmation in all three full-viewport screenshots shows no wrapping. The span height of 15.3 px equals one line of text (line-height 8.87 px) plus vertical CSS padding (0.30 em top + 0.34 em bottom ≈ 4.43 px) plus border — consistent with single-line layout.

### Thai tone/vowel marks

Thai combining marks (tone marks ่ ้ ๊ ๋, vowels above/below ิ ี ึ ื, and clusters like ็ เ แ) are **visible and distinguishable** in all three caption crops at 2× DPR rendering. Characters with stacked marks (e.g., เฝ้า, ได้ยิน) show the diacriticals separated from base consonants.

At 1× DPR the 6.93 px font would put tone marks at ~6 px effective height — at the minimum legibility floor. Modern phones (iPhone, typical Android) render at 2–3× DPR, where the marks are 14–21 rendered pixels and legible. This matches the 4.2 decision's tradeoff note.

### Baseline and contrast

- **Text colour**: #EAF4FF (light blue-white) on translucent backing rgba(20,35,51,.55)
- **Backing style**: Inline-block span with rounded corners (0.42 em), tight padding, border 0.8 px solid rgba(51,72,95,.35)
- **Contrast**: Readable against all three tested scene backgrounds (door close-up, wide washer row with dark floor, mid-shot through glass door). The translucent backing provides sufficient isolation from both light and dark scene elements.

## Limitations

1. **DPR simulation, not physical device**: Screenshots render at 2× device pixel ratio via Puppeteer, which closely matches a typical phone's Retina display but is not identical to on-device rendering (no physical pixel grid, no viewing distance, no ambient light). The 6.93 px effective font on a 1× DPR display would be at the minimum legibility floor for Thai marks.
2. **Headless Chrome vs mobile Safari/WebView**: Font shaping, anti-aliasing, and subpixel rendering may differ slightly on actual mobile browsers (particularly iOS Safari). The Kanit Regular font renders Thai marks correctly in Chrome.
3. **Static frame, not playback**: Each cue was rendered by calling `window.render(t)` at a representative timestamp within the cue's time range, not during video playback. Caption fade/timing transitions are not tested.
4. **textLines calculation in JSON**: The `capture-results.json` reports `textLines: 2` for all cues — this is a **measurement artifact** (spanHeight including padding divided by lineHeight). Visual inspection in all screenshots confirms single-line rendering.
